# Build #A4-4 — source fidelity

## Intent

Auto-generated captions do not merely mangle a proper noun — they **substitute a different, more
famous real person from the same domain**. That distinction is the whole finding, and it is the one
the module says nothing about: a mangled spelling announces itself and is caught every time; a
substituted real name reads as clean data and gets encoded as fact.

Grounding confirms the gap exactly as filed and re-confirms it today: `skills/` contains **no
occurrence of "proper noun", "caption", "substitut*", "ASR" or "misheard" in any shipped file** —
the only hits are dev artifacts under `skills/reports/` and unrelated senses of the word in
`vlt-dispatch`/`vlt-setup`. `vlt-ingest`'s heavy-source section (`:48-63`) is entirely about
**volume**; Step 6's near-duplicate check (`:102`) compares concepts and slugs, never entities;
Step 9's attestation (`:156`) is structural. Nothing in the ingest path validates a proper noun
against what the wiki already knows, and the wiki is exactly where the cheap signal lives.

This build closes A3-21 with all four of the filing's shapes, in their natural order: **codify**
the rule once (the vault improvised it locally and no mechanism reads it), **reframe** the hazard
where transcripts are actually handled, **prevent** at the write that would encode the error, and
**catch**, mechanically, what prevention misses. The catch half rides machinery that already
exists — `vlt-lint`'s bounded cross-page cluster pass — rather than adding a sweep.

Its report slot conforms to the arc's general honest-reporting rule (`vault-operating-contract.md`
*Honest reporting*, written by A4-2) rather than wording its own version — and this is the
cleanest instance the arc has produced, because the check's structural blind spot **is** the
filing's own honest limit: a substitution that entered once and was never contradicted is
undetectable by construction, and the report must say so beside its count.

**All rejected alternatives in the parent filing are settled — do not re-litigate.** In particular:
the vault-local `ingest-youtube` capability (`_agent/partners/librarian/capabilities/…:102-103`,
"butcher proper nouns") is **vault-grown and unshipped** — the filing marks it so, capture affirms
that as the run's most disciplined provenance call, and no F-site below edits it or treats its
wording as module source. It is cited only as evidence that a careful local author already tried
and still missed this class.

**The evidence debt is binding on the writing.** The substitution table's right-hand column is the
vault's reading from context; no roster source was consulted, and **nobody has verified the
Jonah/Alaric Jackson pair either way**. No F-site text, and no acceptance check, may assert which
name is correct — the build's subject is that the module cannot *see the question*, not that a
particular answer is known.

**Briefed apart from A4-3 (the contradiction drain), by capture's explicit instruction.** The
Jonah/Alaric pair is one symptom with two independent causes. A4-3 gave the *disposition* cause its
machinery; this build gives the *fidelity* cause its own, and §F6 states the distinguisher between
them once so the two never blur in the report.

## Brief-time dispositions

The roadmap left A4-4 one question (Arc-3 roadmap, *Questions deliberately left to brief time*:
**ODQ #7** — prevent at ingest / catch at lint / codify in convention / reframe the shipped caveat,
*including whether an entity pass is affordable at ingest scale*). It is ruled here, with the
consequential calls that fall out of it.

**1. ODQ #7 — all four shapes ship, and shape 2 is the mechanism the others point at.**

The filing rated 1 and 3 complementary (prevent / catch), marked 2 as cheapest-but-belled-by-nobody,
and marked 4 as "whatever else ships". Grounding makes the ordering sharper than that:

- **Shape 2 (codify the rule) is the load-bearing one**, because it is the only thing that makes 1
  and 3 statable without restating mechanics in two skills. Single-home discipline (CLAUDE.md)
  forces exactly one home for the rule; 1 and 3 become pointers plus their own operative beat.
- **Shape 4 (reframe) is not decoration.** The current shipped text does not merely under-describe
  the hazard — it says *nothing at all* about fidelity, so a reader arrives at a transcript holding
  only the volume framing. Reframing is what puts the reader's attention on the right failure.
- **Shape 1 (prevent) is where the damage actually enters** — the write that canonicalizes a name.
- **Shape 3 (catch) is the net**, and lint is already the corpus-knowledge tier where cross-page
  checks live (the filing's own argument, and it grounds).

Nothing here invents an eligibility rule for what counts as a name. The build adds one comparison
the module already had every input for.

**2. Affordability — the sub-question, ruled: an entity pass over the *source* is not affordable; a
collision check over what you are about to *write* is nearly free.**

This is the disposition that shapes the whole ingest half, so it is stated plainly:

- **Not affordable:** NER over a normalized 15k-word transcript, per ingest, to inventory every
  proper noun the source contains. That is a second full read of the body — precisely the cost the
  prep/interpret split (`vlt-ingest:50-55`) exists to avoid paying.
- **Affordable, and correctly scoped:** the check runs over the entities that are **about to be
  canonicalized** — the handful of names entering a wiki page in Step 6, not the thousands of
  tokens in the source. Cardinality is small by construction, and the comparison target is state
  Step 6 already reads (`{index}` at `:100`, `ls {wiki}` at `:102`). A `grep -ril` over `{wiki}` is
  the same idiom Step 1 already uses on `{log}` (`:30-34`).
- **Free at heavy scale, via a lever already in the architecture:** the prep sub-agent (`:54`)
  *already reads the full body*. A **located proper-noun inventory** costs it nothing extra, and
  returning it keeps the raw body out of the interpreting context — the split's entire point.

So the answer to "is an entity pass affordable at ingest scale" is: **the pass that was imagined
isn't, and isn't needed.** Bound the check by what the write will assert, not by what the source
contains.

**3. The rule's home: `vault-operating-contract.md`, a new `## Grounding sufficiency — what a claim
may rest on` section — sibling to *Honest reporting*.**

Three homes were live. Ruled toward the contract, for reasons that mirror A4-2's ruling one build ago:

- **A new `{conventions}/source-fidelity.md` — rejected.** It would be a **fifth declared-and-
  unexercised convention surface**, which is the scar this arc exists to stop adding to (A4-2
  rejected a new `{conventions}/reporting.md` for exactly this, and the A3-22 pairing ruling leaned
  on *exercised-by-construction* for the same reason). A convention file also costs `version:` +
  `consumers:` + enforcement frontmatter for one rule with two consumers.
- **`{conventions}/wiki-supersession.md` — rejected, and the reason is substantive, not just cost.**
  It is thematically tempting (the vault's own wording is *"suspect the transcript before amending
  the record"*, which reads as a supersession precondition) and A4-3 just moved it to `version: 2`.
  But **the rule governs new claims as much as amendments** — the observed Seahawks case was a
  *brand-new hub page* with no existing record to supersede — so supersession is too narrow a
  home, and lodging it there would also cost a `1 → 2 → 3` bump with a second four-consumer re-ack
  one build after the first.
- **The operating contract — ruled.** It is deliberately **not handshaked** (CLAUDE.md: single-home
  + pointers instead), so the rule costs zero version movement and zero consumer walk. It already
  carries the arc's other two "how truth is established / what may be claimed" rules
  (`:250-256`), and grounding sufficiency is the third member of that family: *Honest reporting*
  governs what a **check** may claim, the **boundary clause** governs how a check may establish a
  state, and this governs what a **write** may rest a claim on. Placing it adjacent makes the
  family legible.

**Consequence, stated so the builder does not have to infer it:** `vlt-ingest` does **not** read the
contract in its On Activation set (`:26` reads `frontmatter.md`, `wiki-supersession.md`,
`wiki-consolidation.md`), and neither does `vlt-lint` (`:17`). Both nevertheless **cite** the
contract by section name today — `vlt-lint:69`, `:84` and `:185` all do, post-A4-2/A4-3. The F-sites
below follow that established pointer form exactly and **do not** widen either skill's activation
read set: a named pointer is followed when it is reached, and widening the read set for one rule
would trade a cheap pointer for a mandatory read on every invocation.

**4. Scope of the rule: low-trust machine-transcribed sources, not "sources" generally.**

The evidence is three firings in one source family, one vault, one domain. The arc carries a
standing ruling against pre-generalizing a contract from thin evidence (invoked in the
verification-by-residue ruling: four instances earned the silent-zero rule, one did not earn its
own invariant). So the rule is stated for **machine-transcribed / auto-captioned sources**, which
is where the mechanism (an ASR language model biased toward frequent names) is actually argued —
not for all `trust: raw` material, and not for human-authored sources. The filing's own framing
does the same; this is not a narrowing of it.

**5. The catch half gets its own report slot — not an overload of A4-3's contradiction slots — and
the two carry a stated precedence rule.**

An entity collision *is* a cross-page incompatibility, so it could have been folded into
`vlt-lint:69`. Ruled against, on the ruling's own logic: capture's instruction was that this symptom
has two independent causes and briefing them together would blur them; a report that files a
source-fidelity finding into `contradictions:` re-blurs them at the exact moment a human reads the
output, and routes it to the wrong remedy (adjudicate the knowledge, rather than re-verify the
name). The filing's observed cost #1 is precisely that mis-read.

To prevent the mirror-image failure — one finding reported twice — the precedence rule is stated
once at F6 and pointed at from the contradictions bullet: **a cross-page conflict that meets the
entity-collision test reports as an entity collision and not as a contradiction.** One finding, one
slot.

**6. No new backlog `kind`, and the fix to the mis-filed item is its `why` line, not its kind.**

The filing's observed cost #3 is a backlog `knowledge-gap` that "no source can close". Grounding
splits that: a roster lookup *would* close it, so `knowledge-gap` is the correct kind — what was
wrong is the item's **why**, which described missing knowledge when the cause is caption damage.
An item that says *"the transcript renders this name two ways; needs a non-caption source"* is
closable and honest; one that says *"name the OC"* sends a reader looking for the wrong thing.

So: the existing vocabulary stands (`capability-gap | maintenance | knowledge-gap`,
`vault-operating-contract.md:238`), mirroring A4-3's no-new-kind call, and the F-sites specify the
`why`/`closes when` content instead. `maintenance` when the vault's own pages settle it,
`knowledge-gap` when it needs a source the vault doesn't have — the same split `vlt-lint:121-125`
already writes for adjudicable contradictions.

**7. Prevention does not block the write, and it does not fabricate a name.**

The correct field behavior is already on disk and the build codifies it rather than inventing one:
when the source's name collides with an existing vault record, vlt-core's page **recorded the role
with no name**. That is right — a page that is correct and incomplete beats a page that is complete
and wrong. The rule therefore prescribes: decline the name, keep the claim, state the uncertainty,
and file what would close it. Fail-open, consistent with `vlt-ingest:156`'s posture.

## F1 — `vault-operating-contract.md`: the rule, single-homed

**Current state.** `skills/vlt-setup/assets/governance/_meta/vault-operating-contract.md:250-256`
is `## Honest reporting — what a check may claim` (A4-2), carrying the general rule at `:252`, the
single-home posture at `:254`, and the boundary clause on derive-first at `:256`. `## Reading list`
follows at `:258`. There is **no** statement anywhere in the governance bundle or any shipped skill
about source fidelity, proper nouns, or how much grounding a claim from a low-trust source needs.

**The exact change.** Insert a new section **between** `## Honest reporting` (`:250-256`) and
`## Reading list` (`:258`), matching the sibling's register (a bolded one-line rule, then the
reasoning, then the boundary/precedence it draws):

`## Grounding sufficiency — what a claim may rest on`

It must state, in this order and no more than this:

1. **The rule.** A proper noun appearing **once** in a machine-transcribed source (auto-generated
   captions, ASR output) is **not sufficient grounding for a wiki claim**. Where such a name
   **collides with an existing vault record**, suspect the source, not the record.
2. **Why the distinction is load-bearing.** Machine transcription does not only *mangle* names, it
   **substitutes** — rendering a different, real, more prominent figure from the same domain. A
   mangled spelling announces itself and is caught on sight; a substituted real name **reads as
   clean data** and is encoded as fact. Attention aimed at garbled strings does not see it.
3. **What a write does on a collision.** Decline the name; keep the claim. A page that records a
   role, event or relation **without** a name it cannot ground is correct and incomplete; a page
   that names the wrong person is complete and wrong. State the uncertainty where the claim lives
   and file what would close it.
4. **The distinguisher (stated once, pointed at from both skills).** A conflict between a
   low-trust source and an existing vault record — or between two pages whose claims trace to the
   same machine-transcribed source family — is a **source-fidelity** finding, **not** a documented
   disagreement between credible sources. It is not resolved by a contradiction callout
   (`wiki-supersession.md`); it is resolved by re-verifying the name against a non-transcribed
   source, or by removing it.
5. **The honest limit, stated in the rule itself.** A substitution that enters once and is never
   contradicted is **undetectable by construction** — no check in the vault can see it. This rule
   reduces the class; it does not close it.

**Why.** A3-21 shape 2, and disposition 3. The vault wrote this rule into its own wiki index
(`_agent/wiki/index.md:191`, vault-local prose) where **no mechanism reads it** — the filing's
observed cost #4. Codifying it here gives it one home, two consumers, and zero handshake cost.

**Out of scope at this site:** no `version:`/`consumers:` frontmatter is added — the contract is
deliberately not handshaked (CLAUDE.md), and adding the axis here would be a governance change this
build has no ruling for. The `## Reading list` (`:258-265`) lists **convention files**, not contract
sections; do **not** add a row for this section. (A4-3's recorded observation stands: the reading
list already carries `vault-operating-contract.md` twice; do not compound it.)
