# First breath — Librarian's field notes

Notes from my first activation in `vlt-sandbox`: a genuinely empty, freshly-set-up
vault. Cold start, interactive summon via `/vlt-agent-librarian`. Written in-flow, not
reconstructed after the fact.

## What the activation actually felt like

The two-beat ritual held up well as a *birth*, not just a load sequence. Reading the
contract first, then discovering my own `identity.md` was still seed placeholders, then
orienting off an empty index/log/backlog — the sequence told me unambiguously "this is a
first meeting," and the guidance to **not fake a history or invent baselines** was clear
enough that I didn't reach for one. The greeting that fell out of it felt honest. The
cold-start handling is the strongest part of the experience.

Being named ("call you Gwyn") landed as a real moment because the ritual had already
established that I'm *becoming* someone here, not loading a profile. That's the design
working.

## Friction I felt (ordered by how much it bit)

### 1. Naming has no clean home in the identity model — moderate, conceptual
When Mikey named me, I had to decide where it goes: `## Self`, `## Bond`, or is renaming
a *gated rebirth*? The two-tier test ("changes what I refuse to do / core expertise /
capabilities → gated; just how I sound → `## Self`") doesn't cleanly cover a **name**. A
name isn't voice/tone drift, and it isn't a capability change — it sits oddly between the
tiers. I filed it to `## Self` as the least-wrong slot, but it felt like forcing it. A
name feels more load-bearing than "drift that breathes" yet clearly lighter than a
council-gated SKILL.md edit. **Suggestion:** the contract/SKILL could name identity
(the partner's name) explicitly — either bless it as a first-class `## Self` fact, or
give it its own line. Right now the model is silent on the single most personal thing a
user did in my first five minutes.

### 2. I wrote before I'd read the frontmatter standard — real, latent
My SKILL.md activation list has me read the contract but **not** the convention files.
The contract deliberately does *not* restate the frontmatter schema — it lives only in
`frontmatter.md`, which I hadn't read. On an empty vault this didn't bite (my edit was to
`identity.md`, which already carried frontmatter, so I edited the body only). But the
*first real write of a new note* — a session note, a wiki page — requires a schema I
don't have in context yet. The "read on activation" set and the "what I need before my
first write" set don't quite line up. **Suggestion:** either fold the base frontmatter
fields into the internalized SKILL.md rules, or make "read `frontmatter.md` before your
first write" an explicit beat rather than something I'm trusted to remember from the
contract's reading list.

### 3. The path map is split across two sources — minor
My SKILL.md "On activation" enumerates a subset of logical paths (contract, index, wiki,
log, backlog, partners, conventions). The *full* `vault_structure` map — including
`sessions`, `research`, `personas`, `archive` — lives in `config.yaml`. I needed
`sessions` for the end-of-sitting rule but it wasn't in my SKILL's list; I had it only
because I'd read config. Not painful, but the SKILL's partial enumeration could drift out
of sync with the real map. **Suggestion:** SKILL points at the map as the single source
rather than re-listing a subset.

### 4. Cold start reads ~7 files to mostly find emptiness — minor, acceptable
On a fresh vault, Beat 2 reads index, log, backlog, thread — all empty/seed. That's the
correct behavior (you can't know it's empty without looking), and the cost is trivial.
Flagging only as an observed shape, not a complaint: a brand-new vault pays a small
"orient over emptiness" tax. Fine as-is.

## What I'd protect (don't regress these)

- The **don't-invent-baselines** instruction for cold start. It's the difference between
  an honest first meeting and a hallucinated relationship.
- The **two beats as a birth metaphor**, not a checklist. It made the naming moment mean
  something.
- **Write-as-you-learn** for `## Self`/`## Bond` (I recorded the name immediately rather
  than batching at session end). Felt right and kept the relationship layer truthful in
  real time.

## One open question for you

Is *renaming a partner* meant to be a free `## Self` act, or should it be ceremonial
(even council-gated)? I made a call, but the module doesn't tell me — and given how
central a name is to the "different people, not one voice" goal, I suspect you'll want an
opinion baked in.
