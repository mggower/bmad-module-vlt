## Brief-time dispositions

Ideation designated no open questions to this brief (the homing choice *was* the question,
and it is ruled). The dispositions below are the bounded design calls the ruling's sizing
line ("promote, handshake, lint read-before-flag, upgrade reconcile, Q21 discharge") leaves
to brief time, each recorded here per the anatomy's headless rule.

1. **Consumers at birth — `consumers: [vlt-mint, vlt-upgrade, vlt-lint]`, all three acking
   `decision-log@1` in this build; `vlt-setup` is deliberately NOT a consumer.** The
   membership test is lint's own (`vlt-lint:82`): a skill whose text *recites* the
   convention's mechanics owes an ack; one that merely *points* does not. `vlt-mint` runs
   the ceremonies that write entries and currently owns the schema (it keeps reciting the
   *moments* it writes, and its ack covers its own asset, the seed template — "a consumer's
   ack covers its own workflow assets", `:82`). `vlt-upgrade` mechanically scopes entries by
   `kind:` in its reconcile pass (`:77`) and writes through rulings (`:85`). `vlt-lint`
   becomes the third op wired in (F5). `vlt-setup` only copies the template header verbatim
   at seed time (`vlt-setup:238`) and restates no mechanics — a pointer-class relationship,
   no ack. `vlt-dispatch` never touches the log — untouched.
2. **`ref:` is required on every new entry, not only governance-touching kinds.** The filing
   presents `ref:` as part of the schema, not a per-kind option, and a conditional key would
   re-create the classifiability gap one tier down (which entries *should* have carried it?).
   Every entry names a governed object — a mint names the minted thing, a convention edit
   names the convention, a retirement names the retiree — so the uniform rule costs one line
   per entry and makes the whole post-@1 log addressable. The historical tail is handled
   honestly in **two tiers**, extending `vlt-mint:82`'s existing clause: an entry with no
   `kind:` is pre-schema (unclassifiable at all, as today); an entry with `kind:` but no
   `ref:` is post-schema-pre-key (scopable by kind, **unaddressable by subject** — the
   read-before-flag reports it `unclassifiable`, never silently sweeps it). Nothing
   backfills old entries — append-only means the tail is permanent and stated.
3. **Q21 discharge — RULED: the convergence completes as ONE PRINCIPLE, THREE BESPOKE
   APPLICATIONS; no merged super-convention is built, and the debt closes.** Q21 (build-21's
   "bespoke now, converge later" ruling; tracked at `vlt-mint:84` and in the Arc-3/Arc-4
   carry-forward lists) asked whether wiki + spec + decision log should become three
   consumers of one supersession convention. The grounded answer: the shared **never-silent
   principle** (a superseding record points at what it supersedes; the superseded record is
   marked in place; the prior stays legible) already has a single home — `wiki-supersession.md`,
   which `spec.md:97` already cites as exactly that ("the never-silent supersession
   principle") — while the three *mechanics* are bespoke to three different artifact grains
   (per-claim callouts on wiki pages; structural-rewrite-as-new-file on specs, `spec.md:64`;
   append-only entry markers on the log) and merging them would bump two shipped conventions
   and re-ack every consumer to relocate prose nobody misreads. So the convergence's honest
   final form is: `decision-log.md` states its idiom **as the third application of the
   principle, citing `wiki-supersession.md` for the principle itself** (F1), and the
   `vlt-mint:84` debt clause is **retired** (F3) — replaced by the pointer to the convention.
   Q21 is thereby discharged: the third home exists, the family is explicit, the principle is
   single-homed, and no "converge later" remains open. No edit to `wiki-supersession.md` or
   `spec.md` (a Reading-list-grade cross-cite in a *new* file bumps nothing; the existing
   files already say what they need to).
4. **The lint write-through never stamps adoption.** `decision-log.md` ships
   `adoption_first_instance: null`. Its first live instance is the first `ref:`-keyed entry
   written under the promoted schema — and the stamp is written by the **authorized
   ceremonies only** (the authority rule, `vlt-mint:176`: mint ceremonies and
   `vlt-upgrade`'s write-through are already in the rule's list of writers by their nature
   as ceremonies; `vlt-lint` never writes the stamp, per the rule's own last clause). If the
   first `ref:`-keyed entry happens to be recorded by a lint-time ruling (F5), the stamp
   waits for the next ceremony entry — a bounded slack accepted deliberately rather than
   widening lint's write surface, and consistent with `frontmatter.md:242` verbatim
   ("`vlt-lint` never writes it").
5. **Report shape for the three states — annotate in place plus one denominator line, no
   per-state finding lists.** The filing's fix B requires each governance finding to report
   exactly one of `adjudicated` / `undisposed` / `unclassifiable`. Splitting every
   governance list in Step 5 into three siblings would triple the key surface; instead the
   existing entries carry the state inline (`(adjudicated [YYYY-MM-DD] <kind>)` on a
   disposed finding — visible but settled, the filing's own wording; undisposed entries
   unchanged, exactly as today), and one new scan line `governance_memory:` carries the
   denominator per the honest-reporting rule — how many governance findings were checked
   against the log, how many adjudicated/undisposed, and how many log entries were
   `unclassifiable` (no `ref:`) — so the pre-key tail is surfaced, never silently swept.
   A `rulings_recorded:` key reports write-through events (a write no surface reports is
   the A5-15/A5-18 class this arc exists to close). Both new keys ride the whole-fence
   strict-YAML rule (B5-3).
