# ST-8 — always-read memory has no budget and no owner

## The claim

The module's partner model concentrates three failures — activation cost, identity drift, and
inward bias — in **one file, one author, and one loop**, and then couples governance carriage to
that same loop. The owner experiences four complaints (expensive activation, identity that
balloons and cannot be walked back, historical context that boxes thinking inside the vault,
and governance that requires a partner so partnerless sessions feel lawless). They are one
system: the always-read memory has no budget (A) and no owner-side author (B), and the rulebook
rides the persona ritual (C), so the natural escape from A and B is also an escape from the rules.

The contract already solved the wake-read problem **for itself** — a 66K-character contract is
never read on activation; it is distilled to a ~1.1K-word rule-card and opened point-of-use by
section — and never applied the same pattern to partner memory.

rule-card (1,076) + vault rung (145). Full activation adds `## Thread`.

Nine observations the causal chain rests on:

1. **The governance reads are cheap.** Contract-derived rule-card + vault rung + the contract's
   point-of-use sections total under ~1.3K words on activation. The contract is not the cost.
2. **`identity.md` is the cost.** It is the single heaviest Beat 1 read for three of the four
   heavy partners (67–82% of Beat 1), despite being the "evergreen" file the ladder treats as
   the most durable, least churning rung.
3. **`## Self` is the ballooning section.** The football-analyst's `## Self` is ~7,200 words
   under 28 `###` headings, each a dated case narrative in the first person ("I missed a
   pre-commitment I had written myself a date for", "I proposed a call I should not have
   proposed"). It is a casebook, filed in the identity slot because the ladder has no casebook
   rung.
4. **`## Bond` is being used as a user-model store.** The same partner's `## Bond` is ~5,300
   words, mostly domain knowledge *about the owner* ("How he drafts", "Instruments, and how he
   uses them", "The leagues") — material the contract would file in the wiki or a PARA area,
   sitting in Beat 1 because `## Bond` is the only per-partner home for it.
5. **Self-theory loads as instruction.** The Researcher's `## Self` opens with "**The
   correction-of-framing edge — my signature move, six proven target classes**" followed by
   "Target classes 7–13". A partner that has recorded "my edge is correcting the frame" will
   correct the frame. This is the bias mechanism, and it is authored by the partner about itself.
6. **The one cap that exists is hollow.** The Researcher's `reflexes.md` is at cap (30/30) at
   ~1,060 words — ~35 words per "one-line" rule. The cap bounds count, not weight; its own
   frontmatter falsifier predicted this.
7. **Groom is running and not solving it.** The football-analyst was groomed three times
   (2026-08-24, 2026-08-28 ×2; promote 10 / promote 6 / a Bond pass) and stands at ~28.7K words.
   Five of nine partners have never been groomed; groom is invoked-only, one partner per act.
8. **`## Thread` is not bounded in practice.** Beat 2 reads "`## Thread` only", but in the
   heaviest file `## Set aside` begins at line 1,046 of 1,150; the bound excludes ~9%.
9. **The workaround is already in the record.** Session notes tagged `partner: user` and
   `partner: vlt-mint` exist; the owner reports that partnerless brainstorms are the most
   productive sittings, precisely because no partner baggage is present. Those sittings read
   only the seeded `CLAUDE.md` paragraph — no rule-card, no reflex rung, no conventions.

Timeline matters: the owner noticed the cost **before** Cycle 8's memory ladder shipped — the
cost is what prompted the ladder. The ladder addressed *what survives* and left *what must be
read every time* unbounded, so the fix for the symptom built the ratchet.

## The causal chain

**Chain A — cost.** Beat 1 is heavy → because `identity.md` is heavy → because `## Self` and
`## Bond` are ungated and uncapped by design ("drift is free and reversible") while only
`reflexes.md` carries a cap → and groom cannot shrink it because the ladder only lifts (promote
moves material *toward* the always-read file; compress leaves a 500-word narrative per lesson;
retire needs a fired falsifier or a closed item) → because the ladder was designed on one axis,
durability, and the top of that axis is the file read on every activation.

**Chain B — drift and bias.** The identity diverges from what the owner wants the partner for →
because `## Self` is self-authored, in the partner's voice, with no reference to the owner's
intent → and the modulation has outgrown the thing it modulates (base persona 1.7–4.6K words;
accumulated self-theory 3–7K) → and every activation loads the self-theory as instruction, so
behavior confirms narrative and narrative records behavior → and the owner cannot walk it back
because the only exits are groom (upward-biased) and a council rebirth via `vlt-mint`; there is
no reset act and no sanctioned way to summon a partner without its history.

**Chain C — governance.** A partnerless session feels lawless → because it reads only the
`CLAUDE.md` pointer → because the rule-card, the vault rung, and the conventions are wired into
the Activation ritual → because governance carriage was designed when partners were the only
actors, and the sitting is defined as "one partner's turn at the wheel" → so a partnerless
sitting has no name in the contract and nothing was ever wired for it.

**The loop.** R1 (reinforcing): act → record self-lesson → load it → act accordingly; gain rises
with use, so the most-used partners drift fastest. R2 (reinforcing): groom promotes upward → top
rung grows → more to promote. B1 (balancing, leaky): cost + bias push the owner to partnerless
sessions, restoring fresh thinking but venting attribution and the rulebook. B2 (balancing,
expensive): groom / rebirth, one partner at a time, at owner-attention cost, never addressing
weight.

## Rejected alternatives

- **"Run a bigger groom."** Refuted by observation 7. Groom ran three times on the worst case
  and the file is still ~28.7K words, because groom's verbs cannot move material *down*. A
  hygiene act on a one-way ladder is a ratchet with a nicer name.

- **"Activation is expensive because the contract is large."** Refuted by observation 1. The
  contract is point-of-use and its activation footprint is ~1.3K words. Cutting the contract
  would save nothing; cutting `identity.md` is where the weight is.

- **"Governance requires a partner, so decouple the ops from partners."** Half wrong. The ops
  are *already* decoupled (`vlt-ingest` omits the tag when invoked standalone; lint, query,
  extract have no partner gate). What is coupled is the *ceremony* — the rule-card read, the
  reflex rung, session ownership. The fix is a partnerless carrier for governance, not a change
  to the ops.

- **"Retire the partner model; keep the roster as skills."** Refuted by the light partners
  (dog-trainer ~4.4K, health-coach ~5.5K, chess-coach ~7.3K Beat 1), which are cheap, not
  reported as drifting, and carry the longitudinal loops that only a stateful partner can run.
  They are the control group: the model fails at weight, not at concept. A teardown also breaks
  the durability rule (vault-grown identity is never destroyed).

- **"Partners as lenses, not people — memory lives in the wiki and is queried."** The most
  interesting alternative, and closest to the contract's own point-of-use pattern. Rejected as
  the *diagnosis* because it is a fix, and one whose migration destroys the per-partner
  relationship record the durability rule protects. It survives as a candidate for ideation.

- **"This is ST-3 (governance has no addressable projection)."** Adjacent — ST-3 is about paying
  for a whole convention file when a consumer needs a slice. This study's object is not a
  convention but *partner memory*, and the cost is not a projection problem: the file is read
  whole because the ladder put everything durable in it, not because there is no way to address
  a slice. ST-3's remedy (a projection) would not stop R2.

- **"This is ST-4 (provenance staffed, cognition unstaffed)."** ST-4's *written to be re-read
  later, none to be read now* rhymes with observation 3 — the casebook is re-read-later
  material sitting in a read-now slot. But ST-4 is about the factory's lifecycle artifacts; this
  study is about the vault's partner layer, and cause B (self-authorship) has no counterpart
  there.

## What this session got wrong, on record

- The owner's fourth complaint was accepted at face value in the first draft ("most governance
  requires a partner to own the operation"). Grounding showed it half wrong: the *operations*
  run partnerless; only the *ceremony* is partner-bound. The half that was wrong pointed at the
  right fix — the session would otherwise have proposed decoupling ops that were never coupled.

- The first draft attributed the cost to "the ritual." The measurement relocated it: the ritual's
  own reads are ~1.3K words; the partner's self-authored files are 16–20K. A diagnosis written
  before the table would have targeted the wrong file.

- The session initially read the Cycle 8 memory ladder as a *response* to the cost problem that
  had not yet been given time to work. The owner's timeline correction (cost noticed *before*
  Cycle 8; the ladder was the response) reframed it: the ladder is not an untested fix, it is the
  mechanism of R2.

## What would close it

Not stated as a fix — this is a diagnosis, and ideation rules. But the evidence bounds the shape:

- **A second axis.** The always-read set needs a *budget* independent of durability — a declared
  bound the contract states, an instrument (`vlt-vitals`) measures, and a verb (*demote*) groom
  can execute, so that durable material can leave Beat 1 without leaving the vault. The
  contract's own card + point-of-use pattern is the template: a small identity card read always,
  the full record (a casebook; an owner profile in the wiki) opened on demand.
- **An owner-side author.** Something in `identity.md` the partner does not write — a charter of
  what the owner wants the partner *for* — against which self-drift is checked and off-charter
  material demoted; plus a cheap, by-reference reset and a sanctioned cold summon so the owner
  can think without the history without shedding the rules.
- **A partnerless actor.** The contract names the partnerless sitting (note, log tag, write
  bounds), and governance carriage moves to a surface every session reads (the vault
  `CLAUDE.md`, by pointer, never copy), so the owner's escape valve no longer vents the rulebook.
- **Statefulness as a posture, not a birthright** — per partner, opt-in, so the breadth and
  novelty roles can decline the loop the longitudinal roles need.

Whether these ship as one build or three, and in what order, is ideation's ruling. The
diagnosis's own prediction: any fix that leaves the ladder one-way will be re-eaten by growth
within two cycles, because R2 is still running.
